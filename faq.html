General Travel

Yes, most visitors require a visa to enter Sierra Leone. However, citizens of ECOWAS member countries can enter without a visa. For other nationalities, you can obtain a visa on arrival at Lungi International Airport or apply for an e-visa online before your trip. The visa on arrival typically costs $50-100 USD. Make sure your passport is valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay.

The best time to visit Sierra Leone is during the dry season from November to April. This period offers pleasant weather with minimal rainfall, ideal for beach activities, hiking, and outdoor exploration. The rainy season (May to October) can make some roads difficult to navigate and beaches less accessible, though the landscape becomes lush and green during this time.

Sierra Leone is generally safe for tourists, especially in popular areas like Freetown, beaches, and tourist sites. The country has made significant progress since the civil war ended in 2002. However, like any destination, it's important to take standard precautions: avoid walking alone at night in unfamiliar areas, keep valuables secure, and stay informed about local conditions. Most visitors have trouble-free experiences and find the locals to be welcoming and friendly.

The official currency is the Sierra Leonean Leone (SLL). While some hotels and larger establishments may accept US dollars or credit cards, it's recommended to carry local currency for daily transactions, markets, and smaller vendors. ATMs are available in Freetown but may be limited outside the capital. It's advisable to exchange money at banks or authorized exchange offices.

Health & Safety

Recommended vaccinations for Sierra Leone include Yellow Fever (required for entry), Hepatitis A, Typhoid, and routine vaccinations like measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) and diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis (DTP). Malaria prophylaxis is strongly recommended as malaria is present throughout the country. Consult with a travel health clinic 4-6 weeks before your trip for personalized advice.

It's not recommended to drink tap water in Sierra Leone. Stick to bottled water, which is widely available. Use bottled or boiled water for brushing teeth and avoid ice in drinks unless you're certain it's made from purified water. Many hotels and restaurants provide filtered water, but it's always best to confirm.

The main health risks in Sierra Leone include malaria, dengue fever, and waterborne diseases. Use insect repellent, sleep under mosquito nets, and take antimalarial medication as prescribed. Practice good hygiene, eat at reputable establishments, and avoid street food that may have been sitting out. Comprehensive travel insurance that covers medical evacuation is highly recommended.

Transportation

Lungi International Airport is located across the bay from Freetown. You can reach the city by: (1) Water taxi (30-45 minutes, most popular), (2) Ferry (1-2 hours, budget option), (3) Helicopter (10 minutes, expensive), or (4) Road (3-4 hours, scenic but long). Most hotels can arrange transfers. The water taxi is recommended for its balance of speed, comfort, and cost.

Freetown has various transportation options including taxis (negotiate price before boarding), poda-podas (shared minibuses, very cheap but crowded), and motorbike taxis (okadas, fast but less safe). For tourists, hiring a private driver or using ride-hailing apps (where available) is recommended for comfort and safety. Many hotels can arrange reliable transportation for day trips.

Car rental is available in Freetown through international agencies and local companies. However, driving in Sierra Leone can be challenging due to road conditions, traffic, and local driving habits. An international driving permit is required. Most visitors prefer hiring a driver along with the vehicle, which is often not much more expensive and provides local knowledge.

Accommodation & Activities

Sierra Leone offers a range of accommodation options from luxury hotels (Radisson Blu, Bintumani, Swiss Spirit) to mid-range guesthouses and budget options. Beach resorts like The Place at Tokeh Beach and Estuary Resort offer unique experiences. Booking in advance is recommended, especially during peak season (December-April) and holidays. Many accommodations can be booked online through international platforms.

Top attractions include River No. 2 Beach (stunning beach), Bureh Beach (surfing), The Cotton Tree (historic landmark), Tacugama Chimpanzee Sanctuary (wildlife), Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary (nature), Banana Islands (island paradise), and the historic Bunce Island (slave trade history). Freetown offers cultural sites, markets, and vibrant nightlife. Allow at least 5-7 days to explore the main highlights.

Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted at major hotels, restaurants, and some shops in Freetown, but cash is still king. ATMs are available in Freetown and some larger towns, but may not work with foreign cards. It's best to carry sufficient cash (Leone or USD) for daily expenses. Notify your bank of your travel plans to avoid card blocks.

Culture & Etiquette

Sierra Leoneans are known for their hospitality. Greetings are important - always say hello and ask how someone is doing before business. Dress modestly, especially outside beach areas. Ask permission before taking photos of people. Public displays of affection are frowned upon. Respect local traditions and religious practices. Learning a few words in Krio (the local language) will be greatly appreciated.

English is the official language and is widely spoken, especially in business and tourism. Krio is the most widely spoken local language and serves as a lingua franca. Other indigenous languages include Mende, Temne, and Limba. Most people in Freetown and tourist areas speak English well, so language shouldn't be a barrier for travelers.

Sierra Leonean cuisine is flavorful and hearty. Popular dishes include cassava leaves, plasas (stews), fried fish, jollof rice, and groundnut stew. Fresh seafood is abundant along the coast. Fruits like mangoes, pineapples, and bananas are plentiful. Local restaurants offer authentic cuisine, while hotels serve international dishes. Be adventurous but choose places with good hygiene practices.
